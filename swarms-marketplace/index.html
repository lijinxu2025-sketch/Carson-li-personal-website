Swarms Marketplace

Project introduction

Swarms
Marketplace

0–1 product

AI agents are growing rapidly, yet the market still lacks a platform that brings discovery, trust, trial, purchase, and creator incentives into one cohesive experience.

Swarms Marketplace was built as an integrated product ecosystem that supports discovery, trust, trial, purchase, and creator incentives—turning agents into digital assets that can be evaluated, traded, and continuously grow in value. This was a 0-to-1 product initiative.

Problems & Challenges

  1. 1

    Build Product Understanding and Improve Discovery

    Agent, Prompt, Tool, and Tokenized products were presented through inconsistent categories and naming conventions. Users had to understand the platform’s terminology before they could find the right product.

    User Risk
    Users may not understand what the marketplace offers or know where to begin.

  2. 2

    Friction in the Publishing Journey

    The initial publishing experience relied on a dense, single-page form. Critical decisions and standard input fields lacked visual distinction, while categories, tags, and content guidelines were scattered throughout the flow. Users also lacked a persistent preview and clear interaction feedback.

    User Risk
    Users could select the wrong product type, miss required information, or feel uncertain about the final result—increasing rework and abandonment before publication.

Design Goals

  1. 1

    Build Product Understanding and Improve Discovery

    Help users quickly understand the differences between Agents, Prompts, and Tools, while enabling them to find relevant products through multiple discovery paths.

  2. 2

    Strengthen Decision Support and Build Confidence

    Use ratings, usage data, earnings, Creator Reputation, and token metrics to communicate real product value and reduce decision uncertainty.

Competitive Analysis

Desk research

Competitive Analysis Summary

An effective marketplace does more than display every available product. It uses clear categorization, intuitive discovery paths, and scannable product summaries to guide users from understanding and discovery to evaluation and decision-making.

  • First help users understand what is available, then help them determine which option is best.
  • A consistent product model and card structure significantly reduce the effort required to understand and compare products.

Conclusion

In mature marketplaces, users typically already understand the available product types. Agents, Prompts, and Tokenized Products, however, represent emerging offerings without an established mental model. If the homepage leads directly into search, listings, or transactions, users may see the products but still struggle to answer a more fundamental question: What exactly is available here?

Three Cognitive Tasks for First-Time Users

  1. 1 — What Is This?

    Use real product examples to help users understand what the marketplace offers.

  2. 2 — Why Does It Matter?

    Use earnings, popularity, and ratings to help users assess product value.

  3. 3 — What Else Is Available?

    Expand into broader categories and commercial possibilities to establish a complete understanding of the marketplace.

Simplifying the Interface: Prioritizing Core Actions Through Visual Hierarchy

Focus on what matters
Clarity through hierarchy

The early design gave every implementation method equal visual weight. Users had to understand the technical differences before they could continue. My design judgment was simple: publishing should not require users to become protocol experts first.

01 · Cognitive

Users Had to Understand Every Option

Users had to review every option and understand the differences between GitHub, MCP, and x402, creating unnecessary cognitive load.

02 · Behavior

Decision Effort Increased Drop-Off

The complex entry points made publishing feel difficult, reducing users’ motivation to continue.

02 · Rebuilding the Hierarchy with the 80/20 Principle

Core principle

80 / 20

Prioritize the workflow most users already understand while preserving advanced options for expert users.

Primary Path: Import from GitHub

Increase its size, contrast, and visual prominence to create a clear Golden Path that requires no comparison.

Secondary Paths: MCP / x402 / Code

Keep these options accessible while reducing their visual weight to avoid distracting most users.

03 · Complex Capabilities, Simple Entry Points

Reduce Decision Effort

Users can immediately identify the recommended action without reviewing every technical protocol.

Shorten the Task Flow

Users can continue by simply pasting a GitHub URL, turning consideration into direct action.

Support Different User Levels

Everyday users gain efficiency, while advanced users retain access to the full range of integration options.

The goal was not to remove choices, but to give them a clear order.

Clarity → Action → Completion

Positive Feedback: Improving User Control and Visual Validation

Live preview
Immediate validation

I evaluated the early design across three dimensions: interface visibility, visual flow, and feedback timing. The preview feature already existed, but it was not visible when users needed it. The issue was not a missing feature, but feedback appearing in the wrong place and at the wrong time.

01 · Preview Was Disconnected from the Form

→

01 · Visibility

System Status Was Not Visible

The long page hid critical feedback, preventing users from seeing how their product would appear.

02 · Proximity

Inputs and Results Were Too Far Apart

Inputs and preview results were separated, increasing memory load and repeated scrolling.

03 · Feedback

Validation Came Too Late

Issues such as text truncation or missing information were only discovered after users completed the form.

02 · Layout Redesign: Creating a Stable Two-Column Workspace

Wider Editing Area

Provides sufficient space for long-form text, uploads, and configuration tasks.

Fixed-Width Preview

Keeps the product card proportions consistent and prevents layout shifts while scrolling.

One-to-One Field Mapping

Creates a clear spatial connection between each input field and its corresponding area on the product card.

03 · Make Every Input Immediately Visible

04 · UI/UX Design Value

Visibility of System Status

Continuously reflects the current input state, so users never have to wonder whether their changes were received.

Recognition over Recall

Lets users compare inputs directly with the actual card, reducing the need to remember how each field will appear.

Immediate Feedback

Shortens the distance between action and feedback, allowing issues to be identified where they occur.

Error Prevention

Reveals truncation, missing content, and visual imbalance before submission, reducing rework.
