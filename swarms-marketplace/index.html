Swarms Marketplace

Project introduction

Swarms
Marketplace

0–1 product

AI agents are growing rapidly, yet the market still lacks a platform that brings discovery, trust, trial, purchase, and creator incentives into one cohesive experience.

Swarms Marketplace was built as an integrated product ecosystem that supports discovery, trust, trial, purchase, and creator incentives—turning agents into digital assets that can be evaluated, traded, and continuously grow in value. This was a 0-to-1 product initiative.

Problems & Challenges

  1. 1

    Build Product Understanding and Improve Discovery

    Agent, Prompt, Tool, and Tokenized products were presented through inconsistent categories and naming conventions. Users had to understand the platform’s terminology before they could find the right product.

    User Risk
    Users may not understand what the marketplace offers or know where to begin.

  2. 2

    Friction in the Publishing Journey

    The initial publishing experience relied on a dense, single-page form. Critical decisions and standard input fields lacked visual distinction, while categories, tags, and content guidelines were scattered throughout the flow. Users also lacked a persistent preview and clear interaction feedback.

    User Risk
    Users could select the wrong product type, miss required information, or feel uncertain about the final result—increasing rework and abandonment before publication.

Design Goals

  1. 1

    Build Product Understanding and Improve Discovery

    Help users quickly understand the differences between Agents, Prompts, and Tools, while enabling them to find relevant products through multiple discovery paths.

  2. 2

    Strengthen Decision Support and Build Confidence

    Use ratings, usage data, earnings, Creator Reputation, and token metrics to communicate real product value and reduce decision uncertainty.

Competitive Analysis

Desk research

Competitive Analysis Summary

An effective marketplace does more than display every available product. It uses clear categorization, intuitive discovery paths, and scannable product summaries to guide users from understanding and discovery to evaluation and decision-making.

  • First help users understand what is available, then help them determine which option is best.
  • A consistent product model and card structure significantly reduce the effort required to understand and compare products.

Conclusion

In mature marketplaces, users typically already understand the available product types. Agents, Prompts, and Tokenized Products, however, represent emerging offerings without an established mental model. If the homepage leads directly into search, listings, or transactions, users may see the products but still struggle to answer a more fundamental question: What exactly is available here?

Three Cognitive Tasks for First-Time Users

  1. 1 — What Is This?

    Use real product examples to help users understand what the marketplace offers.

  2. 2 — Why Does It Matter?

    Use earnings, popularity, and ratings to help users assess product value.

  3. 3 — What Else Is Available?

    Expand into broader categories and commercial possibilities to establish a complete understanding of the marketplace.

Discovery Path Redesign

Transform visually appealing content cards into product cards that support evaluation and comparison—helping users understand what the Marketplace offers and find the right products faster.

Card anatomy / 信息分层

  1. Surface Product Identity First

    Place status, product type, and per-use pricing directly in the cover area to answer the first question at a glance: “What is this?”

  2. Maintain Visible Ownership and Trust Signals

    Position the creator’s avatar, name, and verification status close to the title to establish clear ownership and trust.

  3. Highlight Differentiating Value

    Adapt earnings, capability tags, and token performance to each product model, making the reason to purchase immediately visible.

  4. Create a Consistent Comparison Baseline

    Keep ratings, review counts, and runs in a consistent footer position to create a shared basis for cross-product comparison.
