Overview

What it is & why it’s complex

Pendle is a decentralized finance protocol that turns future yield into a tradable asset — letting users separate, price, and trade the yield of an underlying token independently from its principal.

The complexity sits in the primitives. Principal Tokens (PT), Yield Tokens (YT), maturity dates, implied vs. underlying APY, liquidity depth, and the risk attached to each must all be legible at the moment of decision — or the product is unusable to anyone but quants.

System & Problem

A clean system that’s hard to hold in your head

Pendle splits any yield-bearing token in two: a Principal Token you redeem at maturity, and a Yield Token that captures the stream of yield until then. Both parts trade on an open market, so their prices imply a rate — the market’s live opinion on future yield. Users can lock in fixed yield, long or short future rates, or provide liquidity.

That same machinery is what made it intimidating: before committing capital, users had to hold several abstractions in their head at once and trust their own math.

  • PT and YT values drift in non-obvious ways as maturity approaches.

  • Implied APY vs. underlying APY — easy to confuse, costly to misread.

  • Maturity, slippage, and liquidity all affecting the real outcome of a trade.

  • Risk that had to be communicated honestly without scaring users away.

Decision · V2 launch

Put education inside the product, not just in the docs

When Pendle V2 was about to launch, the default was the industry default: ship the app, explain it in the docs. Nearly every protocol worked that way, and the team saw no reason to differ — anyone serious would go read the documentation, so explanations inside the app itself were seen as unnecessary.

I disagreed with the premise. The people who most needed to understand PT and YT were exactly the ones who would never open a docs site before their first trade — they would guess, or leave. Rather than argue it through, I built it: an education layer inside the app that walks from a token the user already holds to “you can trade its two halves.” The first version was close to what ships today.

The signal came from outside. A wave of positive reactions on X cited the in-app education specifically as the reason Pendle felt approachable and trustworthy next to other DeFi protocols. A “nobody does this” became something users talked about unprompted — and set the precedent that in Pendle, teaching is part of the product surface, not a footnote.

Decision · Get Started

Move education into the flow itself

The first education layer worked, but it lived beside the app rather than inside it — you could learn, then trade, but they were two separate rooms. The data made the gap visible: new-user retention was low. People arrived, never got oriented inside the flow, and left before their first position.

The response was the Get Started page set — education rebuilt as part of the UX rather than a destination. Instead of sending a newcomer away to learn first, the pages meet them where the decision happens: what a market is, what they are choosing between, and what happens next, inline with the act of getting started.

The first version got the idea right and the proportions wrong. The markets section — the part that actually matters to a newcomer — was too small relative to the rest of the layout; every step was explained in text with no images; and there was no FAQ, so the same questions kept surfacing anyway. The revision rebalanced the layout around markets, added an image explaining each step, and added an FAQ for the recurring questions. That is the version in the app now.

Get Started covers the first trade. Not everyone learns at that moment, though — so the whole path beyond it is designed like a product rather than a pile of links: in-app explainers, docs, video, and Pendle Academy all sit behind one hub, organized by difficulty so users ramp at their own pace.

At the deep end sits the written reference. Pendle Academy turns the documentation into a curriculum — Beginner to Advanced — so the depth becomes a path someone can walk, not a wall of pages to search.

Abstraction

Simplicity for everyone else

The other user doesn’t want a lesson — they want the outcome. For them, the complexity is folded away: routing, wrapping, and intermediate tokens collapse into a single action; smart defaults handle the machinery; advanced controls sit one layer deeper, out of the way until asked for.

The result: a trade that touches five contracts reads as one decision — and neither user is forced into the other’s experience.

Product Translation

System → usable experience

I translated the protocol into flows, screens, and data visualization that let a user understand a position before they take it — turning math into a decision they can read at a glance.

Website & Marketing Design

The front door to the protocol

Beyond the app, I designed and shipped the Pendle marketing site — the landing page that gives new users their first impression and gives partners and press their reference point.

The same care extends to marketing: an illustration system for launches, partnerships, and campaigns — every piece drawn in one visual language, so each announcement compounds the brand instead of diluting it.

Visual & Brand System

A language built for trust

I built Pendle’s brand identity as a system: a logo suite, typography, and a tokenized color palette that keep a high-stakes financial product precise, restrained, and confident on every surface it touches.

Codified and published as a public media kit, it’s the shared source of truth that internal teams, partners, and press all build from.

To keep that language alive beyond my own hands, I built Pendle Canvas — a design tool that turns the system into a library anyone on the team can compose with, on brand by default.
